# Mesh moon, macOS icon build

Blender 5.2, Cycles. Everything is generated by `blender-flat-moon.py`. The scene follows the way the Linear icon is built: flat layers on a plate, an orthographic camera straight on, one broad soft key from the upper left.

## Layers

All heights are in plate units, where the plate half-width is 1.0 and the camera ortho scale is 2.28, the same frame as the earlier Linear studies.

| Layer | Top height | Thickness | Bevel | Material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plate | 0.0 | 0.070 | 0.020 | matte night, gradient `#0c0e15` to `#161a27` |
| Night disc | 0.022 | 0.022 | 0.004 | graphite blue, metallic 0.48, roughness 0.18, coat 0.8 |
| Rear sheet | 0.0265 | 0.0045 | 0.0008 | dark bronze, metallic 0.35, roughness 0.5 |
| Front sheet | 0.0335 | 0.0045 | 0.0012 | amber gold, metallic 0.72, roughness 0.30, coat 0.3 |

The disc radius is 0.66. A shallow dome of 0.030 is added across the disc and both sheets after the pores are verified, so the gloss reflection reads as one broad band and the layers stay parallel. The gap between the sheets is 0.0025, close to the 2.5 px separation used in the gloss screen study.

## Crescent and pores

The sun vector is the ASCII moon's key light, `(0.8, 0.3, -0.3)` normalised. The crescent outline is the lit, visible part of the unit sphere projected onto the plane: the visible half of the terminator circle joined to the lit arc of the limb, 720 samples.

The pores come from a latitude/longitude grid on the sphere with its pole at the sun, 96 longitude segments by 24 rings from the terminator to the pole, 3.75 degrees each way. Every cell whose four corners face the camera is projected to the plane and inset by half the strut width. Cells that collapse under the inset, fall below the minimum pore size, sit within one and a half struts of the limb, or lose convexity are dropped. That leaves a solid rim along the limb and a density gradient toward it, the same gradient the ASCII moon shows.

The rear sheet uses the same grid shifted by 0.32 of a cell in both directions and a narrower strut, so it shows through the front pores as offset bronze bars.

| Sheet | Cells facing camera | Pores kept | Blocked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Front | 655 | 388 | 0 |
| Rear | 640 | 415 | 0 |

Each sheet is a 2D curve with the outline as the outer ring and the pores as inner rings, extruded, bevelled, converted to a mesh. Before the dome is applied, a ray straight down through every pore centroid must miss the sheet. All 803 pores pass.

## Shading

The 2D fill produces long thin triangles from the outline to the nearest pores. With the dome applied, interpolated vertex normals streaked across them. The script sets custom normals: the analytic dome normal on cap faces, straight down on the underside, and the computed normal on walls and bevels. Shading is smooth by angle at 35 degrees so caps and walls stay distinct.

## Lighting and camera

- Key: broad softbox upper left, 300 W, 4 by 3 units, slightly cool.
- Warm gold: upper right, 120 W, light-linked to the two sheets so it never prints on the disc.
- Cool lower fill: 30 W.
- Reflection: a 3.4 by 0.26 strip upper left rolled 38 degrees, 95 W, the diagonal reflection from the gloss study.

World grey 0.10 for a touch of ambient. Camera orthographic at scale 2.28 for the icon, 1.45 for the mark, 0.46 for the detail. AgX view transform with the Punchy look, 192 samples with denoising. Each render takes three to four seconds on the Metal GPU.

## Verification

The icon was inspected at 1024 and downscaled to 128, 64 and 32 px. The crescent, the terminator edge and the blue disc all survive at 32 px. The pore pattern reads as texture from 128 px down and as individual cells at 1024.

Earlier in this build, Blender's curve fill capped nearly every front pore. The cause was inset polygons that inverted on foreshortened cells near the limb, which broke the fill for the whole contour set. Strict convexity and containment checks on the inset fixed it. Two remaining slivers at the limb were closed by the bevel and are now excluded by the limb margin.

## The sphere study

`sphere/` keeps the first pass: a real sphere with lattice shells and a three-quarter hero. It is too deep and too blocky for a macOS icon, but the sun-aligned lattice idea carried over into this build.
